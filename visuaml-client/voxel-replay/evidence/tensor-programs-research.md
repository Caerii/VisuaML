# Tensor Programs and neural assemblies in VisuaML

VisuaML’s existing composed inference trace gives two separate experimental signals. The hard-attention interpreter matched the scalar oracle on all 14 executed calls in the controlled-English tool-acquisition evaluation. The learned controller chose the expected tool on 12 of 16 requests. When the first result changed from 10 to 15, the next request copied 15 into the argument and retained the old tool. A causal-transformer lowering would change how the exact transition is implemented. A Tensor Programs analysis would ask how the learned routing machinery behaves as its width and head count change. ## Three levels of claim

| Experiment | Claim to test | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Finite-width tensor assembly | A fixed causal decoder realizes a declared state transition within its admitted domain. | A refinement argument over every source boundary, a numeric error bound, and replay against the exact executor. |
| Learned routing | The learned controller uses the live result and the tool descriptions to bind the correct handle. | Matched intervention pairs, held-out aliases and registry permutations, selection margins, and separate argument and outcome scores. |
| Wide learned lane | Selected activation and routing statistics approach a limit under a stated scaling regime. | A width and head-count sweep and, for a theorem, a derivation under the applicable Tensor Programs assumptions. |

Greg Yang’s Tensor Programs I derives Gaussian-process limits at initialization for architectures under stated scaling and regularity assumptions. Tensor Programs IV studies infinite-width training in a parameterization that preserves feature learning. Attention requires care even at initialization. A shared random attention matrix can yield a non-Gaussian single-head limit. A suitable many-head limit gives a Gaussian law in the setting analyzed by Hron and colleagues. A result for a fixed token horizon leaves the behavior at growing horizon as a separate question. [Tensor Programs I](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12478) · [Tensor Programs IV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.14522) · [Infinite Attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10540)

Start with open-loop measurement. Supply a fixed recorded history of tool results to every width and measure learned-lane preactivation covariance, tool-logit margins, and accuracy over independent seeds. Vary learned width and head count separately while holding the exact lane, cases, precision, and token horizon fixed. Record parameterization, fan-in scales, attention-logit scale, normalization, and tied weights. Compare closed-loop runs separately. Autoregressive selection and feedback make the next input depend on previous model outputs. A proof for that regime must account for the adaptive dependency and the discreteness of token selection, including near ties.

## An exact lane beside a learned lane

An engineered assembly can be a declared group of coordinates carrying a typed value, such as a program counter, address bits, write payload, or execution status. A tensor assembly language would specify the permitted readers and writers, the state invariant, and the lowering to fixed weights. Its recurrent proof relates each admitted causal history to a reference machine state. Initialization establishes that relation, each generated record preserves it at the declared source boundary, and halt or fuel exhaustion limits later updates. This is a finite-width argument, with an additional numeric margin for any discrete decisions recovered from softmax.

A model with separate exact and learned lanes can make that invariant easier to audit. Put the boundary at a validated request and a typed result. Audit normalization, Q/K score inputs, residual writes, and output decoding as well as projection weights. Whole-residual LayerNorm couples channels through their shared mean and variance even with block-diagonal projections. Dedicated heads and separate normalization or a documented bypass let a checker establish which coordinates can affect the exact circuit. Prove that two executions with equal admitted request tensors and program images generate equal exact traces even if their learned activations differ.

A learned assembly is a separate hypothesis about causal mediation in the controller. Patch a candidate result-bearing subspace with activations from the matched counterfactual run and predict which tool request should change. Test whether removing the subspace suppresses the expected switch and restoring it rescues the switch. Include matched-size random and unrelated-coordinate controls, preserve nuisance factors such as registry ordering, and measure off-target changes in arguments and protocol. Repeat across seeds and widths. Papadimitriou and colleagues' assembly calculus provides a precedent for computation by neuronal populations with specific recurrent dynamics, competition, and plasticity. Evidence for an engineered residual group or a learned subspace has its own scope. [Brain computation by assemblies of neurons](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2001893117)
